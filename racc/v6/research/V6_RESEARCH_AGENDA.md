# ZeroStack RACC Draft 6 Research Agenda

This agenda is separate from the archival papers.

## Formal proof priorities

1. finite harness decision-boundary trace projection;
2. exact longest-common-prefix rewrite economics;
3. causal DAG invalidation and equality-boundary cutoff;
4. Q99 residency budget and eviction slack;
5. multi-resource feasibility intervals;
6. atomic project-state refinement;
7. finite decision-view quotient and exact expansion;
8. successor-state protected simulation.

## Empirical priorities

1. adaptive semantic-decision depth across real coding tasks;
2. causal-lens completeness and counterexample rate;
3. one-/two-call coverage;
4. L1 miss insulation with L2 warm and L3 cold/warm;
5. dependency-weighted invalidation mass;
6. time/work to restore Q99 after change;
7. physical capacity needed for L3 Q99;
8. protected regression and strict-rescue rate;
9. complete-work frontier closure;
10. background CPU/RAM and long-lived daemon feasibility.

## High-value unresolved questions

- Does mature repository work have sufficiently sparse conditional novelty for fallback mass to approach zero?
- Are task-relative causal closures small and certifiable enough to dominate repeated transcript search?
- Can exact equality boundaries stop most invalidation after ordinary refactors?
- How much local storage is required to maintain L3 Q99 under realistic demand and drift?
- Can capability assets deliver positive lifetime value after capture, verification, maintenance, and revocation costs?
- Which protected dimensions can be automatically verified, and which require human authority?
- Can provider prefix-cache stability and ZeroStack append-only capsules produce multiplicative rather than overlapping savings?

## Stop rule for new headline theorems

A new top-level theorem enters the current release only if it:

- changes a runtime authority decision;
- provides a tighter unavoidable-work lower bound;
- yields a finite checker or certificate;
- reduces a measured Frontier Closure burden;
- explains a reproducible implementation failure;
- or proves an impossibility that prevents wasted engineering.

Other mathematical observations belong in notes until implementation evidence shows they are load-bearing.
